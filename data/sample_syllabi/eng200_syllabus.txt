COURSE DESCRIPTION

This course introduces students to the methods and practices of literary
analysis across genres including fiction, poetry, and drama. Students will
develop critical reading skills, learn to construct evidence-based arguments,
and communicate interpretive claims through both written essays and oral
presentations. Texts span from the 19th century to the contemporary period.

REQUIRED TEXTBOOKS

Eagleton, T. (2008). Literary Theory: An Introduction, 3rd ed. Blackwell.
Bressler, C. (2011). Literary Criticism: An Introduction to Theory and Practice,
5th ed. Pearson.

Course Reader — available at the campus bookstore (selected poems and short fiction).

ASSIGNMENT DETAILS

Essay 1 — Close Reading (Week 4, 15%)
Write a 1,200–1,500 word close reading of a single poem or short passage from
the course reader. Focus on how formal elements (imagery, syntax, meter)
contribute to meaning. Estimated preparation: 2 weeks of drafting and revision,
approximately 12 hours total.

Oral Presentation (Week 6, 15%)
Deliver a 10-minute presentation analyzing a secondary text of your choice from
the course reading list. You must use visual aids and provide a one-page handout
to the class. Followed by 5 minutes of Q&A. Estimated preparation: 1.5 weeks,
approximately 10 hours including research, slide design, and rehearsal.

Essay 2 — Comparative Analysis (due 2025-10-27, 20%)
Write a 2,000–2,500 word essay comparing two texts from different genres studied
in class. Apply at least one theoretical lens (e.g., feminist, postcolonial,
structuralist). Expect roughly 18 hours of effort spread over 3 weeks.

Research Essay Proposal (due 2025-11-10, 10%)
Submit a 500-word proposal for your final research essay, including a working
thesis, preliminary outline, and annotated bibliography of at least 4 scholarly
sources. Plan for about 8 hours of library research and writing over 2 weeks.

Essay 3 — Research Essay (due 2025-12-01, 25%)
Write a 3,000–3,500 word research essay engaging with scholarly criticism on a
text or author from the syllabus. Must incorporate at least 6 peer-reviewed
sources. This is the capstone assignment. Start early; expect 25–30 hours of
work over 4 weeks including research, drafting, and revision.

FINAL EXAM (2025-12-15, 15%)
A three-hour sit-down exam with passage identification, short-answer questions,
and one essay question. Covers all texts and theoretical frameworks from the
term. Budget 2 weeks of review, approximately 12 hours of study time.

ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

offense under university policy.
